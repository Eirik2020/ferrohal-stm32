# Plan: the STM32F411, F722, F745 and G474

Opened 2026-11-17. The owner's fifth and last priority of 2026-11-17: add the STM32F411,
the F722 and the F745 to FerroHAL. It runs beside the open work only where it does not
collide with it. When it is done this plan is deleted, after its facts have moved to the
documents that own them (`docs/CHIPS.md`, `docs/design/glue-generator.md`, the IP modules).

## What the chips carry

Read from stm32-metapac 21.0.0's metadata, the version `Cargo.lock` pins (measured
2026-11-17, one part per line; the other packages of each line carry the same versions).

| peripheral | F401 / F405 (driven) | F411 | F722, F745 | F767 (on the rig) | H743 / H753 (driven) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| USART | usart_v2 | usart_v2 | **usart_v3** | usart_v3 | usart_v4 |
| SPI | spi_v2 | spi_v2 | **spi_v3** | spi_v3 | spi_v4 |
| I2C | i2c_v1 | i2c_v1 | i2c_v2 | i2c_v2 | i2c_v2 |
| RCC | rcc_f4 | rcc_f4 | **rcc_f7** | rcc_f7 | rcc_h7rm0433 |
| DMA | dma_v2 (streams) | dma_v2 | dma_v2 | dma_v2 | dma_v1 + DMAMUX |
| IWDG | iwdg_v1 | iwdg_v1 | iwdg_v2 | iwdg_v2 | iwdg_v2 |
| ADC | adc_v2 | adc_v2 | adc_v2 | adc_v2 | adc_v4 |
| GPIO, EXTI, timers | gpio_v2, exti_v1, timer_v1 | the same | the same | the same | gpio_v2, exti_h7, timer_v3 |
| USB | otg_v1 | otg_v1 | otg_v1 | otg_v1 | otg_v1 |
| SD card | sdmmc_v1 (no driver) | sdmmc_v1 | sdmmc_v1 | sdmmc_v1 | sdmmc_v2 |
| flash, power | flash_f4, pwr_f4 | the same | flash_f7, pwr_f7 | the same | flash_h7, pwr_h7rm0433 |

The die IDs are 0x431 (F411), 0x452 (F722, F723, F732, F733) and 0x449 (F745, F746,
F756); the F767 on the rig is 0x451.

So the F411 is a new die and nothing else: every IP version it carries is already driven
and proven on the F401. The F722 and the F745 share I2C v2 and IWDG v2 with the H7, and
DMA, ADC, GPIO, EXTI, the timers and USB with the F4, and bring three IP versions
FerroHAL does not drive:

- **usart_v3** is usart_v4 without the PRESC register and without the FIFO bits; every
  other register sits at the same offset. FerroHAL's usart_v4 code writes PRESC once
  and uses no FIFO.
- **spi_v3** is spi_v2 with a FIFO: CR2 gains the data size (DS), the receive threshold
  (FRXTH) and the DMA odd-length bits, SR gains the FIFO levels, and DR is read and
  written as 8 or 16 bits. An 8-bit frame needs FRXTH set and byte-wide DR access.
- **rcc_f7** is rcc_f4's tree plus the over-drive mode 216 MHz needs (in the PWR), more
  flash wait states, and the kernel-clock muxes in DCKCFGR1 and DCKCFGR2 (the UARTs,
  I2C, the 48 MHz clock).

The F7 is a Cortex-M7 with caches, like the H7. Its streams reach the DTCM through the
core's AHBS port (RM0431 and RM0385 2.1.6, AN4667 1.3.3), unlike the H7's, so
`check_dma_buffer` takes the DTCM as never cached and applies the MPU rule to SRAM1 and
SRAM2.

The F722, F745 and F767 modules exist (`rcc_f7`, `spi_v3`, `chips/stm32f722re.rs`,
`chips/stm32f745vg.rs`, `chips/stm32f767zi.rs`, `chips/shared/f7.rs`, the ES0360, ES0290
and ES0334 tables), for every package of each line, with `i2c_v2`, `iwdg_v2` and the
F4's IP versions behind them. What they still lack:

- **The UART:** usart_v3, inside the UART redesign (step 4 below). Until then the F7 has
  no `uart` module and its glue no `usarts!` rows.
- **No-panic:** `checks/no-panic` is built around a UART, so `checks/run.sh` skips it on
  the F7 and says so. The F7 joins it with its UART, after the redesign rewrites that
  program.
- **Bench proof:** the Nucleo-F767ZI's bench code and wiring (below); the procedure
  stub is `requirements/benches.md`, SPI on the Nucleo-F767ZI.

## The STM32G474

Added by the owner on 2026-11-17, for the Nucleo-G474RE on the rig (die 0x469). Measured
in metapac 21.0.0 (stm32g474re; the CE and VE carry the same versions):

- **Already driven:** usart_v4 (as the H7), i2c_v2 and iwdg_v2 (as the H7 and F7), gpio_v2,
  exti_v1 (as the F4).
- **Shared with the F7:** spi_v3, so either the G474 or the F767 can prove it.
- **New:** rcc_g4, timer_v2, adc_g4, and USB as the full-speed device peripheral (usb_v3
  with its own packet RAM), not the OTG core the other chips have, so `synopsys-usb-otg`
  does not serve it.
- **New DMA:** channels (bdma_v1) behind DMAMUX1, not streams. Every DMA-driven driver
  (UART, SPI, ADC, the timer lanes) is written against stream DMA today. bdma_v1 is the
  same IP version as the H7's BDMA, so an engine for it would also reach the H7's SPI6 and
  LPUART1.

The G474 is a bench part on the rig, not a flight controller part. It needs the most new
code of the four.

## The generator

The glue generator took the F411 with no change beyond its registry row:
`glue-gen emit part stm32f411ce` cuts die 0x431's routing to the UFQFPN48's 36 bonded
pins, and adds SPI4 and SPI5, which the F401 lacks. The F7 parts' offering policy in
`tools/glue-gen/src/emit.rs` drives spi v3 and leaves usart_v3 out until the redesign.

## Evidence

Under the car rule one chip proves an IP version for every family, and one chip a
family's shared glue.

- **F411:** all its IP versions are proven by the Nucleo-F401RE, but that does not lift
  it: a capability is the weaker of its IP evidence and its die axis, and the die axis
  needs a verified glue probe (GLUE-001) on a board of the same die (`maturity.md`).
  Without an F411 board on a debugger every capability reads – and the platform `INH`
  (measured: `glue-gen tiers`, 2026-11-17). A flight controller flashed over DFU reaches
  runs at `FLSH` but not the glue probe, which needs a debugger.
- **F722, F745:** the Nucleo-F767ZI on the rig carries every IP version they carry, and
  the F767 is now a supported line, so it can prove usart_v3, spi_v3, rcc_f7 and the F7
  series glue; for the same reason as the F411, the F722 and F745 read – until a board of
  each die runs the glue probe. Its USB port (CN13) is not connected today, and
  bench code waits for the owner's go.

## Bench wiring, proposed

Worked out on 2026-11-17 from UM1974 Table 19 (the Nucleo-F767ZI's Zio headers; its
morpho footprints are not fitted, as on the H753), UM2505 Tables 15 and 16 (the
Nucleo-G474RE's Arduino and morpho headers), and metapac's alternate functions. Every
link is a plain DuPont jumper from a TX or MOSI to its own RX or MISO, as on the H753. Each
bench's list moves into its `board.rs` header, and the Pico rows into `hil/WIRING.md`, when
its bench code is written. Not yet checked on the boards: the default solder-bridge states,
which the text extraction of both manuals loses (their bold).

**Nucleo-F767ZI.** Pins kept free: the Ethernet RMII pins (PA1, PA2, PC1, PC4, PC5, PG11,
PG13, and PA7 and PB13 behind JP6 and JP7), USB on PA9 to PA12 and PG6/PG7, the LEDs on
PB0, PB7 and PB14, the button on PC13, SWD and SWO (PA13, PA14, PB3), and the ST-LINK MCO
on PH0. USART3 (PD8, PD9) reaches the ST-LINK's virtual COM port through SB5 and SB6, so
it needs no jumper, as the F401's USART2 does.

| link | carries | pins | Zio sockets |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | USART2 | PD5 -> PD6 | CN9-6 -> CN9-4 |
| F2 | USART6 | PG14 -> PG9 | CN10-14 -> CN10-16 |
| F3 | UART4 | PD1 -> PD0 | CN9-27 -> CN9-25 |
| F4 | UART5 | PC12 -> PD2 | CN8-10 -> CN8-12 |
| F5 | USART3 or UART4 | PC10 -> PC11 | CN8-6 -> CN8-8 |
| F6 | UART7 | PE8 -> PE7 | CN10-18 -> CN10-20 |
| S1 | SPI1 or SPI3 | PB5 -> PB4 | CN7-13 -> CN7-19 |
| S2 | SPI2 | PC3 -> PC2 | CN9-5 -> CN10-9 |
| S3 | SPI4 | PE6 -> PE5 | CN9-20 -> CN9-18 |
| S4 | SPI5 | PF9 -> PF8 | CN9-28 -> CN9-24 |

The car rule needs one UART on each APB bus (F1 and F2: each kernel mux of rcc_f7) and
one SPI (S1); the rest prove the die's routing rows on the wire, as the H753's full set
does. USB: the CN13 cable, with JP4 on (UM1974 7.10, which also says the board must be
powered before CN13 is plugged).

**Nucleo-G474RE.** Pins kept free: LPUART1 (PA2, PA3) on the ST-LINK's virtual COM port,
SWD (PA13, PA14), SWO on PB3 (SB15), PB8, which is BOOT0 when the option bytes take it
from the pin, the HSE crystal on PF0/PF1 and the LSE on PC14/PC15. PA5 drives the LED
LD2, which an SPI clock tolerates.

| link | carries | pins | sockets |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | USART1 | PC4 -> PC5 | CN9-2 (D1) -> CN9-1 (D0) |
| G2 | USART3 | PB10 -> PB11 | CN10-25 -> CN10-18 |
| G3 | UART4 | PC10 -> PC11 | CN7-1 -> CN7-2 |
| G4 | UART5 | PC12 -> PD2 | CN7-3 -> CN7-4 |
| S1 | SPI1; TIM3 CH2 into CH1 | PA7 -> PA6 | CN5-4 (D11) -> CN5-5 (D12) |
| S2 | SPI2 | PB15 -> PB14 | CN10-26 -> CN10-28 |
| A1 | a GPIO into ADC1/ADC2 IN1 | PA1 -> PA0 | CN7-30 -> CN7-28 |

S1 also carries the timer lanes and samplers, as the F401's does: timer_v2 is new. A1
gives adc_g4 a driven input. The G474's USB is not offered for now (owner, 2026-11-17).

**Picos.** The one Pico 1 serves the F401 (I2C0) and the H753 (I2C1) and has no I2C block
left. A second Pico 1 running the same simulator, with its own bus tags, serves the F767
and the G474:

| Pico 2 pin | board | signal |
|---|---|---|
| GP0 | F767 CN7-4 (PB9) | I2C1 SDA |
| GP1 | F767 CN7-2 (PB8) | I2C1 SCL |
| GND | F767 CN7-8 | ground |
| GP2 | G474 CN10-1 (PC9) | I2C3 SDA |
| GP3 | G474 CN10-2 (PC8) | I2C3 SCL |
| GND | G474 CN10-9 | ground |

The G474 uses I2C3 rather than I2C1 on D15/D14, because I2C1's SCL is PB8 (BOOT0). Two
more Picos would also cover rig items already waiting on one: B1 (the instrument Pico)
and A4 (a second I2C controller) in `hil/RIG-IMPROVEMENTS.md`. USB ports: each new board
needs its ST-LINK cable, the F767 its CN13 cable, and each Pico its own cable.

## What collides with open work

PR #49 (branch `debt-code-items`) touches every chip module, the generated glue, the
shared macros, `emit.rs`, `lib.rs` and `Cargo.toml`, and still owes the open-drain
`Alternate` type, the I2C glue rows and the `pins!` macro. The UART single-owner
redesign rewrites `src/uart/`, `usart_v2/` and `usart_v4/`. `hil-speedups` touches the
bench scripts. This plan's work is therefore cut from `debt-code-items` and split:

1. **Now, beside #49:** this plan; the F411's registry row and its generated glue; the
   roadmap line in `CHIPS.md`.
2. **Now that the manuals are in `docs/`, and the datasheets once they are** (RM0383, RM0431, RM0385, RM0410 and
   their errata sheets ES0287, ES0360, ES0290, ES0334): the F411's chip module, its hand
   facts (clock limits, RAM, USB FIFO, calibration addresses) and its errata table, and
   its Cargo feature. Rebased onto #49 once it merges.
3. **After #49 merges:** rcc_f7, spi_v3, i2c_v2 and iwdg_v2 on the F7, the F7's shared
   glue (`chips/shared/`), and the F722, F745 and, if chosen, F767 parts.
4. **After the UART redesign lands:** usart_v3, inside the redesigned UART.
5. **When the owner gives the go for bench code:** the F767 and G474 benches, wired as
   proposed above, and the second Pico's simulator build.

**The F7 code and the UART redesign.** The F7 modules touch four files the redesign
also rewrites, each by a line or two: the `spi_v3` gates in `src/spi/mod.rs`, the
`uart` gate in `src/lib.rs`, a gate on `AnyStream::alias` in `src/dma_stream.rs` (the
redesign removes `alias`, so its side wins), and `resource.rs`'s `Refused` test, which
now names an SPI error. Whichever lands second takes these by hand.

**Evidence.** Evidence follows files, so the F7 change also drops the F4 and H7 tiers that
cover the shared files it edits (`spi/mod.rs`, `dma_stream.rs`, `i2c_v2`, `rcc_f4`,
`resource.rs`, the H7 chip files): measured with `glue-gen tiers` on the branch. The
redesign drops most of them too; one bench pass after both lands restores them.

## The owner's decisions

Asked and answered on 2026-11-17.

- **Q1, the parts:** stm32f411ce (UFQFPN48), stm32f722re (LQFP64) and stm32f745vg
  (LQFP100), the packages flight controllers use, plus the development board on the rig.
  FerroHAL's focus is flight-controller coverage and the rig's boards.
- **Q2, evidence:** the Nucleo-F767ZI becomes a supported part and the F7's proof part.
  The F411 stays at `INH`; no F411 board will be bought. (Corrected the same day: with
  no die-0x431 board its capabilities read –, not `INH`; see Evidence.)
- **Q3, module layout:** option A. A new `rcc_f7` module on the shared RCC code, a thin
  `spi_v3` module on the shared `spi/` code, and usart_v3 served by the usart_v4 code
  with only its PRESC write gated, settled inside the UART redesign.
- **Q4, manuals:** the owner added RM0383, RM0431, RM0385, RM0410 and ES0287, ES0360,
  ES0290, ES0334 to the workspace's `docs/`, sorted into a folder per chip, then the
  datasheets DS11853 (F722), DS10916 (F745) and DS11532 (F767), PM0253, AN4667 and
  UM1974, and DS10314 (F411), which the F411's clock limits and calibration addresses
  cite.
- **Q5, packages (2026-11-17):** every package and flash size of the supported lines is
  a part, not only the one picked in Q1 (`design/glue-generator.md`). Dies with no
  board stay unproven; the owner does not intend a board per die.
- **Q6, more lines (2026-11-17):** the F446, F427/F429, F412, F413, F410, F76x/F77x and
  H750 join the work list at the same last priority, all unproven; the H72x/H73x do
  not. Each needs its reference manual, datasheet and errata sheet in `docs/`.
  The F446, F427, F429, F412 and F413 modules exist (`chips/stm32f446re.rs`,
  `chips/stm32f427vi.rs`, `chips/stm32f429zi.rs`, `chips/shared/die_0x419.rs`,
  `chips/stm32f412zg.rs`, `chips/stm32f413zh.rs`, the ES0298, ES0206, ES0305 and
  ES0372 tables) on
  the F4's IP versions, with the over-drive that `rcc_f4` now enters above a chip's
  threshold (RCC-004). The rest of the list is open, in the order above.
