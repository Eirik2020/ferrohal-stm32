# Plan: the STM32F411, F722 and F745

Opened 2026-11-17. The owner's fifth and last priority of 2026-11-17: add the STM32F411,
the F722 and the F745 to FerroHAL. It runs beside the open work only where it does not
collide with it. When it is done this plan is deleted, after its facts have moved to the
documents that own them (`docs/CHIPS.md`, `docs/design/glue-generator.md`, the IP modules).

## What the chips carry

Read from stm32-metapac 21.0.0's metadata, the version `Cargo.lock` pins (measured
2026-11-17, one part per line; the other packages of each line carry the same versions).

| peripheral | F401 / F405 (driven) | F411 | F722, F745 | F767 (on the rig) | H743 / H753 (driven) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| USART | usart_v2 | usart_v2 | **usart_v3** | usart_v3 | usart_v4 |
| SPI | spi_v2 | spi_v2 | **spi_v3** | spi_v3 | spi_v4 |
| I2C | i2c_v1 | i2c_v1 | i2c_v2 | i2c_v2 | i2c_v2 |
| RCC | rcc_f4 | rcc_f4 | **rcc_f7** | rcc_f7 | rcc_h7rm0433 |
| DMA | dma_v2 (streams) | dma_v2 | dma_v2 | dma_v2 | dma_v1 + DMAMUX |
| IWDG | iwdg_v1 | iwdg_v1 | iwdg_v2 | iwdg_v2 | iwdg_v2 |
| ADC | adc_v2 | adc_v2 | adc_v2 | adc_v2 | adc_v4 |
| GPIO, EXTI, timers | gpio_v2, exti_v1, timer_v1 | the same | the same | the same | gpio_v2, exti_h7, timer_v3 |
| USB | otg_v1 | otg_v1 | otg_v1 | otg_v1 | otg_v1 |
| SD card | sdmmc_v1 (no driver) | sdmmc_v1 | sdmmc_v1 | sdmmc_v1 | sdmmc_v2 |
| flash, power | flash_f4, pwr_f4 | the same | flash_f7, pwr_f7 | the same | flash_h7, pwr_h7rm0433 |

The die IDs are 0x431 (F411), 0x452 (F722, F723, F732, F733) and 0x449 (F745, F746,
F756); the F767 on the rig is 0x451.

So the F411 is a new die and nothing else: every IP version it carries is already driven
and proven on the F401. The F722 and the F745 share I2C v2 and IWDG v2 with the H7, and
DMA, ADC, GPIO, EXTI, the timers and USB with the F4, and bring three IP versions
FerroHAL does not drive:

- **usart_v3** is usart_v4 without the PRESC register and without the FIFO bits; every
  other register sits at the same offset. FerroHAL's usart_v4 code writes PRESC once
  and uses no FIFO.
- **spi_v3** is spi_v2 with a FIFO: CR2 gains the data size (DS), the receive threshold
  (FRXTH) and the DMA odd-length bits, SR gains the FIFO levels, and DR is read and
  written as 8 or 16 bits. An 8-bit frame needs FRXTH set and byte-wide DR access.
- **rcc_f7** is rcc_f4's tree plus the over-drive mode 216 MHz needs (in the PWR), more
  flash wait states, and the kernel-clock muxes in DCKCFGR1 and DCKCFGR2 (the UARTs,
  I2C, the 48 MHz clock).

The F7 is a Cortex-M7 with caches, like the H7: the DMA buffer check needs the same
non-cacheable rule, and its tightly-coupled data RAM is reachable by DMA on the F7,
unlike the H7's (to be checked in RM0431 and RM0385 before it is written).

## The generator

The glue generator took the F411 with no change beyond its registry row (2026-11-17):
`glue-gen emit part stm32f411ce` cuts die 0x431's routing to the UFQFPN48's 36 bonded
pins, and adds SPI4 and SPI5, which the F401 lacks. The F7 parts need the offering
policy in `tools/glue-gen/src/emit.rs` to learn the three new IP versions, which is
written once their drivers exist.

## Evidence

Under the car rule one chip proves an IP version for every family, and one chip a
family's shared glue.

- **F411:** all its IP versions are proven by the Nucleo-F401RE, so it reaches `INH`
  with no board of its own. Die 0x431 is proven only by an F411 board (a Nucleo-F411RE
  would do; a flight controller would reach `FLSH` at best). None is on the rig.
- **F722, F745:** the Nucleo-F767ZI on the rig carries every IP version they carry. If
  the F767ZI becomes a supported part, it can prove usart_v3, spi_v3, rcc_f7 and the
  F7 series glue; the F722 and F745 dies stay `INH` without boards of their own. Its
  USB port (CN13) is not connected today, and bench code waits for the owner's go.

## What collides with open work

PR #49 (branch `debt-code-items`) touches every chip module, the generated glue, the
shared macros, `emit.rs`, `lib.rs` and `Cargo.toml`, and still owes the open-drain
`Alternate` type, the I2C glue rows and the `pins!` macro. The UART single-owner
redesign rewrites `src/uart/`, `usart_v2/` and `usart_v4/`. `hil-speedups` touches the
bench scripts. This plan's work is therefore cut from `debt-code-items` and split:

1. **Now, beside #49:** this plan; the F411's registry row and its generated glue; the
   roadmap line in `CHIPS.md`.
2. **Now that the manuals are in `docs/`, and the datasheets once they are** (RM0383, RM0431, RM0385, RM0410 and
   their errata sheets ES0287, ES0360, ES0290, ES0334): the F411's chip module, its hand
   facts (clock limits, RAM, USB FIFO, calibration addresses) and its errata table, and
   its Cargo feature. Rebased onto #49 once it merges.
3. **After #49 merges:** rcc_f7, spi_v3, i2c_v2 and iwdg_v2 on the F7, the F7's shared
   glue (`chips/shared/`), and the F722, F745 and, if chosen, F767 parts.
4. **After the UART redesign lands:** usart_v3, inside the redesigned UART.
5. **When the owner gives the go for bench code:** the F767 bench, and an F411 bench if a
   board is bought.

## The owner's decisions

Asked and answered on 2026-11-17.

- **Q1, the parts:** stm32f411ce (UFQFPN48), stm32f722re (LQFP64) and stm32f745vg
  (LQFP100), the packages flight controllers use, plus the development board on the rig.
  FerroHAL's focus is flight-controller coverage and the rig's boards.
- **Q2, evidence:** the Nucleo-F767ZI becomes a supported part and the F7's proof part.
  The F411 stays at `INH`; no F411 board will be bought.
- **Q3, module layout:** option A. A new `rcc_f7` module on the shared RCC code, a thin
  `spi_v3` module on the shared `spi/` code, and usart_v3 served by the usart_v4 code
  with only its PRESC write gated, settled inside the UART redesign.
- **Q4, manuals:** the owner added RM0383, RM0431, RM0385, RM0410 and ES0287, ES0360,
  ES0290, ES0334 to the workspace's `docs/`, sorted into a folder per chip. Still asked
  for: the datasheets (DS10314 for the F411, the F722's, DS10916 for the F745, DS11532
  for the F767), PM0253 for the Cortex-M7, and UM1974 for the Nucleo-F767ZI. Clock
  limits wait for the datasheets.
